World Clock — Current Time Around the World

See the live local time in cities across the globe. Add the places you care about, and VClock keeps every clock accurate — including daylight saving — automatically.

Why a World Clock Matters

Remote teams, family abroad, international markets and global sports fixtures all run into the same problem: "what time is it there right now?" A world clock answers that instantly, and more importantly it answers it correctly — including the daylight saving shifts that catch people out twice a year.

VClock reads time zone rules from the IANA database built into your browser, the same source operating systems use. That means Phoenix stays on Mountain Standard Time all year while Denver switches, London and New York change DST on different weekends, and India stays 30 minutes offset from its neighbours — all without you configuring anything.

In the United States, daylight saving currently begins on the second Sunday in March and ends on the first Sunday in November. Hawaii and most of Arizona do not observe it at all, which is why a call that works in July may not work in January.

Scheduling Across Time Zones Without Mistakes

  • Always name the zone. "3 PM ET" removes ambiguity that "3 PM" creates.
  • Watch the date line. A Friday afternoon meeting in New York is Saturday morning in Sydney — check the date on each card, not just the hour.
  • Beware the DST gap. For three weeks in March and one in November, the US and Europe are one hour closer or further apart than usual. Recurring meetings drift during these windows.
  • Find the overlap first. Add every participant's city, then look for hours that sit between roughly 9 AM and 6 PM on all cards at once.
  • Use UTC for records. Logs, deadlines and contracts are far less error-prone when written in UTC.

How Time Zones Work

The world is divided into zones offset from Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), the modern successor to Greenwich Mean Time. In theory each zone is one hour wide; in practice borders follow political lines, and several regions use 30- or 45-minute offsets — India is UTC+5:30, Nepal UTC+5:45, and parts of Australia UTC+9:30. Add seasonal daylight saving, which about a third of the world observes on its own schedule, and manual conversion becomes genuinely error-prone. That is exactly the arithmetic a world clock removes.

World Clock FAQ

Is the world clock accurate?

Yes. Times are calculated from your device clock using the official IANA time zone database built into your browser, which includes current daylight saving rules for every region.

Does it handle daylight saving time automatically?

Yes. Because it uses your browser's time zone engine, DST transitions are applied automatically — you never need to adjust anything in March or November.

Can I add my own cities?

Yes. Choose a city from the dropdown and press Add. Your list is saved in your browser, so it is still there next time you visit.

How many cities can I track?

As many as you like. The grid reflows to fit your screen, and each card updates every second.

Why do some cities show a different date?

Time zones can be more than 24 hours apart in practice, so when it is Tuesday morning in New York it is already Tuesday evening in Tokyo. Each card shows its own local date to make this obvious.

Is this useful for scheduling meetings?

Very. Add every participant's city and you can see at a glance which hours fall inside everyone's working day, without converting anything by hand.
